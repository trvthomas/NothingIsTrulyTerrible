A visual web experience based on Rafael Santandreu's book *Nada es tan terrible*, part 1.

## Overview

Nothing Is Truly Terrible is an interactive web experience that brings Rafael Santandreu's renunciation and creation ideas to an English-speaking audience. Not through translation, but through visuals. Built with parallax layers, illustrated scenes, and a narrative that moves from the storm to the sunlight, the experience guides users through Part 1 of Santandreu's book. Each scene has its own visual identity, mood, and reflections that represents the journey from mental weight to everyday joy.

## The Book

*Nada es tan terrible* (or how I decided to translate it, Nothing is Truly Terrible) is a self-therapy book by Spanish psychologist Rafael Santandreu, currently available in Spanish, Catalan, and Portuguese. Part 1 introduces his core philosophy: most human suffering is self-invented; a tangle of mental constructions he calls *"neuras"* that we mistake for reality. His solution to these inventions initially consists of two moves: renunciation, the ability to detach from anything without losing wellbeing, and creation, the ability to find a new frame of joy in every situation. Through metaphors like the monkey trap, the water glass, and the all-bloom garden, Santandreu argues that happiness is not something we find... it was always there, we were just looking in the wrong place.

## My Process

The project started with a concept: I wanted to translate and share Santandreu's ideas from Spanish to English. And I wanted to do it in a visual language that anyone could experience. From there, the process had four stages.

**Concept and research:** I reread *Nada es tan terrible* and took notes on Part 1, identifying the core ideas, metaphors, and emotional progression. The goal was to find which concepts were visual by nature and build the experience around those moments rather than around chapters or page counts.

**Screen mapping:** With the ideas mapped, I defined 15 unique screens, each with its own topic, mood, color palette, and visual metaphor. The sequence was designed to feel like a journey rather than a slideshow, opening in storm and heaviness, moving through tension and release, and closing in warmth and openness. Each screen's layout, gradient direction, and element composition was carefully selected, sketched, and planned before any code was written.

**Design and asset creation:** Every visual element was designed or reviewed by hand. Over 150 unique assets including floating elements, typographic cards, frame borders, background compositions, and scene-specific decorations. All assets were designed with CorelDRAW and Corel PHOTO-PAINT. For full details on originality and AI use in elements, see the AI Usage section inside the experience.

**Development:** The site was built using HTML, CSS, and Vanilla JavaScript, with [Parallax.js](https://github.com/wagerfield/parallax) handling the 3D depth layers. Every design decision made during the previous three stages directly informed how the site was coded: structure followed story, not the other way around.
